What makes it feel professional

  • A real 3D flip with a slight springy overshoot, so it feels physical rather than a flat swap.
  • A visible stack: the next cards peek out behind the current one, so you can tell there are more to go.
  • Swipe with feedback: the card tilts as you drag and shows an AGAIN or GOOD stamp before you let go.
  • Rating buttons appear only after you flip, so you can't rate a card before seeing the answer. Each button shows when you'll see the card next.
  • A structured back: definition, why it matters on the exam, an example and a source.
  • Respects Reduce Motion: if you turn that on in iOS settings, the animations are toned down.

Libraries for the real app

  • Motion (formerly Framer Motion) for the flip, drag gestures and spring physics. The same library also handles page transitions and the answer panel on the question screen.
  • ts-fsrs for scheduling. It uses FSRS, the same algorithm modern Anki uses, and it's more accurate than the older SM-2 algorithm.
